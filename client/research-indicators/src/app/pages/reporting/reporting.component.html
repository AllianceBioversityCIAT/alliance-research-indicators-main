What are you reporting?

Hover a card for more details.

Outputs:

what we produce

When: When formally published or officially released.

Evidence: DOI, publication or repository record.

Avoid: Drafts, duplicates, incomplete metadata.

When: After delivery; multi-session programmes count as one.

Evidence: Participant list, agenda, materials, workshop report.

Avoid: Meetings or awareness events with no learning component.

When: When an innovation reaches a documented readiness level.

Evidence: Testing, validation, trial or prototype documentation.

Avoid: Reporting activities instead of innovations; overstating readiness.

Outcomes:

the change that follows when others use it

When: When identified users actively apply the innovation.

Evidence: Adoption data, monitoring records, user confirmations.

Avoid: Confusing dissemination, training, or awareness with use.

When: When research uptake, enactment, or impacts are evidenced.

Evidence: Policy documents, legal instruments, implementation evidence.

Avoid: Reporting consultations or advocacy without evidence of change.

When: When research contributed to measurable change.

Evidence: Evidence of outcome and Alliance contribution.

Avoid: Reporting activities instead of outcomes or impacts.

What was the primary funding source?

The stream that financed most of the activities, resources and staff time behind the result.

Not sure how the CGIAR funding streams work? Start here

Pooled — W1 / W2

W1 supports the CGIAR Portfolio as a whole; W2 is designated to specific CGIAR Programs and Accelerators.

Results reported in PRMS.

Non-pooled — W3 / bilateral

W3 is donor funding allocated to a single Center through the CGIAR Trust Fund; bilateral funding flows directly from donor to Center. Both are Center-managed project funding.

Results reported in STAR.

Finding your primary source

Follow the money: the stream that financed the majority of what made the result possible —

  • staff time dedicated to achieving the result;
  • research, implementation, engagement and partnership activities;
  • travel, workshops, training events and field costs;
  • the technical inputs most critical to the result.

Mixed funding is normal. Pick the largest contributor — STAR and PRMS both let you record the other contributing projects and funders.
