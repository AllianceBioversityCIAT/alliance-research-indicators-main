What are you reporting?

Hover a card for when to report, evidence and pitfalls

Outputs

what we produce

When: Once the product is published or publicly available.

Evidence: DOI, repository or publisher link.

Avoid: Registering the same product twice.

When: After delivery; multi-session programmes count as one.

Evidence: Participant list, agenda, materials, workshop report.

Avoid: Meetings or awareness events with no learning component.

When: The current readiness level can be justified by evidence.

Evidence: Technical docs, prototypes, testing or validation results.

Avoid: Reporting planned work or overstating the level.

Outcomes

the change that follows when others use it

When: Identified users have applied, or are applying, the innovation.

Evidence: Adoption surveys, monitoring data, analytics, user confirmations.

Avoid: Treating training, availability or awareness as use.

When: Stage 1 uptake, Stage 2 enactment or Stage 3 impact.

Evidence: Drafts or approved instruments, budgets, minutes, evaluations.

Avoid: Advocacy or consultations with no documented change.

When: There is credible evidence of measurable change.

Evidence: Policy documents, monitoring data, evaluations, adoption stats.

Avoid: Describing activities instead of the change.

What was the primary funding source?

The stream that financed most of the activities, resources and staff time behind the result.

Not sure which one? Follow the money

Pick the stream that covered most of:

  • staff time dedicated to the result;
  • research, implementation or partnership activities;
  • travel, workshops, training and field costs;
  • the technical inputs most critical to the result.

Several funders? Choose the largest contributor — STAR and PRMS both let you record the other contributing projects.
